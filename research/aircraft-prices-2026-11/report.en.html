Buying converts money into an asset; leasing creates a recurring payment. Flight economics needs an allocation of ownership to aircraft use. For a leased aircraft, divide monthly rent by monthly block hours, then multiply by the flight's block time. A purchased aircraft needs a separate capital and residual-value model; its purchase price cannot replace monthly rent.

What “after” means

For five new-aircraft types, the age matches the formula’s basis. Their rent can be anchored to a dated reference: retain the source range and use its midpoint for one scenario. The resulting factor belongs to a particular type, age, period and set of conditions. It neither makes the formula universal nor validates future rent forecasts.

Lafter,type,age,t₀ = Lmodel,type,age,t₀ × k
k = [(Llow,t₀ + Lhigh,t₀) / 2] / Lmodel,type,age,t₀

The short mark spans only the source’s dating precision. Its agreement with that source is built into the calculation, not an independent test. The “after” line is not extended beyond it. ATR and E175 have no correction: comparable rents for aircraft around age twelve are needed.

Charts use the original line’s fixed 1.16 USD/EUR convention. The flight example below uses the 1.113 USD/EUR snapshot. Thus the A320neo chart correction is about +0.9%, while EUR cost per flight changes about +5.2%; the difference includes FX conversion.

The type selects one of two generation groups, and maximum take-off weight scales its base rate. This formula has no separate segment coefficient, seat-count term or continuous age curve. How aircraft price becomes a lease rate

The lessor pays for an aircraft now, receives rent over time and retains an asset to re-lease or sell. Rent must cover value loss, expenses and the required return on capital. A constant percentage of catalogue price cannot determine it: actual acquisition cost, term, future value, contract conditions and the market matter.16

The formula below calculates required rent under stated assumptions. A negotiated market rate can differ. Air Lease's 2025 filing describes fixed rates on existing leases and separate interest or manufacturer-escalation adjustments for future deliveries. A change in borrowing rates does not automatically rewrite an existing lease.17

How the payments were derived: formulae, terms and accounting

P + C₀ = Σt=1…N (L − C)/(1+i)t + (S − Csale)/(1+i)N
i = (1+r)1/12 − 1; aN = [1 − (1+i)−N]/i
L = C + [P + C₀ − (S − Csale)/(1+i)N] / aN

Future amounts are first discounted to today: a dollar in ten years has a different weight from a dollar now. P is acquisition price, C₀ upfront costs, C monthly owner expenses, S assumed resale and Csale selling costs. r is the required effective annual pre-tax asset return, not the bank-loan interest rate. N is lease months. This version assumes month-end payments.

Who receives cash, and what counts as profit

Now add financing separately. A bank lends 70% of purchase price at a 6% effective annual rate. Payments amortize over fifteen years, but the loan matures in ten; outstanding principal must be repaid at sale. Equity funds the remaining price and upfront costs. This is an illustrative financing structure, not a bank quote.

If all assumptions hold, equity earns 12.2% annually before tax. Borrowing raises the return here because financing costs less than the asset earns. It also creates mandatory payments and greater equity-loss exposure. The monthly 176,910 USD is not net accounting profit.

Profit ≈ rental revenue − costs − depreciation − interest − taxes
Equity cash flow = rent − cash costs − interest − principal repayment

For an accounting illustration, assume a 25-year useful life and USD 7.5m residual at the end of that life. This differs from a USD 25m resale after ten years. Upfront acquisition costs are capitalized. First-year depreciation is 1,720,000 USD and interest 2,004,538 USD. Profit before tax and other items is 1,906,621 USD, while equity cash after bank payments is 2,122,924 USD. Principal repayment reduces cash, not accounting profit; depreciation does the reverse.

How a transaction is arranged

An aircraft may come from a lessor's manufacturer order, the secondary market, or a sale-and-leaseback: an airline sells it and leases it back. Lufthansa disclosed such 2025 transactions for fifteen A320-family aircraft over 72 months and four A350s over 144 months. Term and residual exposure are contract-specific.19

StepWhat is agreed and checked
1. Aircraft and counterpartyvariant, delivery date, condition and records; airline credit and maintenance capability
2. Heads of termsletter of intent: term, rent, deposit, aircraft reservation and key return conditions
3. Contract and financingdetailed contract, insurance, security, permitted operation, defaults and closing conditions
4. Acceptance and lease startaircraft and records inspection, delivery conditions, purchase payment and documented handover
5. Operation and returnpayments, maintenance reserves and condition monitoring; then extension, return, re-lease or sale

This is a general sequence; the signed contract sets specific obligations. An aircraft sale between owners can transfer the existing lease without requiring the aircraft to leave its airline's fleet.16,18,20

Payments that must be separated

Basic rent pays for use of the aircraft. A security deposit supports performance, with repayment governed by the contract. Maintenance reserves accumulate for agreed events and reimbursements depend on eligibility. Return-condition compensation can also arise. A received deposit or reserve is therefore not entirely free owner profit.16,18

The calculator needs separate views of flight cost and cash requirements. If reserves are paid and later reimburse the same maintenance, counting both in full duplicates that cost. Ownership and leasing need different capital models. Leasing also does not imply no balance-sheet obligation for the airline: IFRS 16 provides for right-of-use assets and lease liabilities, with the standard's exceptions.21

Separate aircraft age from the market year

A ten-year-old aircraft being cheaper than a new one does not isolate depreciation. Valuation date, variant, specification and appraiser must be held comparable. Comparing an older aircraft in a crisis with a new one in a shortage mixes age and market conditions. We form comparisons within type, period and source.

No owner documents are needed for this stage; a useful later check would be shareable lease terms with date, age and payment scope.

From aircraft price to the cost of a flight

The purchase price is not charged in full to a flight. Under a leasing scenario, ownership per flight = monthly rent ÷ monthly aircraft utilisation × flight block hours. Fuel, airport fees, crew and maintenance are separate; a passenger fare is not the same as flight cost.

Questions about aircraft prices and leasing

How much does a new Airbus A320neo cost?

The cited IBA appraisal for a new A320neo in Q1 2026 is approximately USD 56.3 million. This is neither a disclosed transaction price nor a manufacturer list price.

How much does an Airbus A321neo cost to lease?

For a new A321neo (A321-200NX), IBA appraised monthly lease rates at USD 456–488 thousand in Q1 2026. Actual contracts vary by age, configuration, customer and maintenance terms.

What does one flight or a passenger trip cost?

There is no universal price: route, fuel, airport charges, crew, maintenance, rent, utilisation and occupancy all matter. The EUR 2,508–2,692 here is only the lease component of an illustrative A320neo flight. Use the calculator for total cost and break-even fare.
